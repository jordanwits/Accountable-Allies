2. About Patti (the person we’re selling)

The single most important thing to internalize before you write a line of code: Patti is the product. The existing site reads as “competent corporate” — indistinguishable from every faceless online bookkeeping service. Patti’s competitive advantage is that she is a real, experienced, warm human who picks up her own phone. Your job in this build is to make that obvious on the page within five seconds of arrival.
What we know about her:
Decades of experience as a strategic bookkeeper/accountant.
Meticulous by nature — she keeps records the way her future self will thank her for.
Believes “sales” is fake — i.e., convincing someone to do what they don’t want. She will not tolerate a hypey, pushy tone anywhere on the site.
Treats clients better than she’s been treated. Her tagline-energy: she wants people to exhale when they read her words.
Strict about her scope (see Section 5) — she does NOT want the site to suggest she does anything outside strategic bookkeeping.
Has a dry sense of humor and tells good stories about herself — see Section 3, these are the heart of the copy.
Her own framing for all of this — and it should anchor the homepage and About page — is the line she opens her BNI talk with: “I’ve done life the hard way.” The three stories in Section 3 are the substance behind that claim. She closes the same talk with: “Because I’ve done things the hard way, I’ve learned how to make things simpler and less overwhelming for others.” Build the homepage and About page so they ladder up to that closer.

3. Patti’s stories — the heart of the copy rewrite

These three stories came directly from Patti and are stories she loves telling. They are the centerpiece of the About page and should be threaded through the rest of the site where they fit. Each one does specific work — humanizing her, illustrating a value, or demonstrating her competence without her bragging.
Story 1 — The IRS audit (“Ignorance is not bliss”)
Early in Patti’s career, she and her husband had a 1099 from a business that didn’t make any money, so she didn’t file it with the IRS. The IRS audited her anyway. The agent told her she likely owed $1,900 and said the now-famous line: “Ignorance is not bliss.” But because Patti’s records were meticulous, when the dust settled she actually walked out with an additional refund — not a bill.
Why it earns its keep: this is the single best story in the set. It’s self-deprecating (she’s the one who got audited), it teaches the lesson she sells (good records protect you), it has a memorable hook (“Ignorance is not bliss”), and it ends with the kicker that her competence turned a bill into a refund. She doesn’t have to claim she’s good — the story does it for her.
Where to use it: About page, prominently. Could also be teased on the homepage as a credibility moment. This is the one we lead with.
Story 2 — High school algebra (“The only thing that matters is the BOTTOM LINE”)
Patti consistently got the right answers on her algebra tests but didn’t show her work, so she was failing. She asked her teacher why she was failing when her answers were right. He said it was because she didn’t show her work. Her response (and the line she loves): “The only thing that matters is the bottom line.”
Why it earns its keep: it positions Patti as pragmatic and results-oriented — she’s not going to perform process theater. For her clients, the bottom line is what matters; she does the meticulous showing-the-work behind the scenes so they don’t have to. There’s a productive tension with Story 1 (the audit) that resolves nicely: she shows her work for the IRS, not for the client. The client just sees clarity.
Where to use it: About page (paired with Story 1), or as a homepage callout/pull-quote near “How working with me actually goes.”
Story 3 — The mortician aptitude test
In college, Patti took an aptitude test that told her she’d be great as either a mortician or an accountant. Dead bodies give her the heebie-jeebies, so she went the accountant route.
Why it earns its keep: pure humanization. Light, self-aware, funny, and easy to read in her voice. It lowers the temperature and makes her feel approachable before the heavier stories land.
Where to use it: open the About page with this. It sets the tone before Stories 1 and 2 do the heavier lifting.
4. Ideal client (Patti’s growth target)

Patti has two concrete growth targets she’d like the new site to attract. Every design and copy decision should serve at least one of them:
Medium-sized businesses — auto shops, ~10-employee operations. Target: 3–5 of these.
Small operators — cleaners, handymen, similar service shops. Target: 8–12.
Common thread: owner-operators who have been burned by a sloppy bookkeeper or ghosted by an online service. They specifically want a real human they can call. The site should make them feel like they’ve already met Patti by the time they fill out the contact form.
From Patti’s BNI talk, a more granular picture of who she’s a fit for:
Service-based business.
A few years in — not brand new.
Has activity — real revenue, real transactions; not a side project.
Feels unsure about their numbers.
Or just doesn’t want to be the one handling the accounting on top of everything else.
Referral partners (new to the build)
In her BNI talk, Patti named her preferred referral sources: CPAs, financial advisors, lenders, and insurance agents. We hadn’t discussed this in earlier meetings, but it’s worth a small beat on the site — even a sentence or two on the homepage or Contact page — signaling that these introductions are welcome. Confirm with Patti whether she wants this surfaced explicitly before building it in (see Open Questions).
5. “What I do / What I don’t do” — required content block

Patti was emphatic about this in our meeting. The site must explicitly draw the line on her scope — both to attract the right clients and to repel the wrong ones. Display this as a side-by-side block somewhere prominent (homepage and/or Services page).
Phrasing for the live block can be Patti’s voice (“Here’s what I do, and here’s what I won’t pretend to do…”) — the table above is the substance, not the literal copy.
6. Strategic positioning — the one big idea

Sell Patti, not a category. The current site competes on the same dimension as every churn-and-burn online bookkeeping service: “we’re competent and corporate.” That’s a fight she can’t win, and it gives away the only thing the competition can’t copy — her.
Concretely, this means:
Patti’s real photo on the homepage hero. Not stock. Warm, unfussy — at her desk, with coffee, looking like someone you’d call.
Her first name on the homepage within the first viewport.
First person (“I”, “my”) throughout. Never “we.”
Copy that sounds like she said it out loud — contractions, plain language, no jargon, no hype.
Trust signals: testimonials with real first names + towns, credentials (QuickBooks ProAdvisor and anything else — confirm with Patti).
7. Voice & tone guidelines

First person. “I keep your books accurate.” Never “we” or “Our team.”
Contractions allowed and encouraged — “I’ll,” “don’t,” “you’ve.”
Zero hype. No exclamation points (or one, maybe, in a 4-page site). No “unlock your potential,” “game-changer,” “seamless,” “solution.”
Warmth + competence. The reader should exhale when they land on the page. “Someone’s in charge here and she’s not going to make me feel dumb.”
Sentences that sound spoken. Read every line out loud during copy review; if it doesn’t sound like Patti could say it, rewrite it.
Calm CTAs. “Schedule a 20-minute conversation” — not “Get started today!”

If Patti is comfortable using this on the site (it’s her story, no named client), it would anchor a short “What working with me actually looks like” section. Confirm with Patti before placing it — see Open Questions.
9. Design direction

The visual feel we’re after is a well-kept professional office — warm wood, good light, neat papers — not a SaaS dashboard.
Color
Anchor: deep navy or forest green.
Background: warm cream / soft off-white. Avoid pure white.
One accent, used sparingly: muted gold, terracotta, or sage.
The current palette (cool blues, pure white) is too cold and template-y — deliberately move warmer.
Typography
Headlines: a serif — traditional but not stuffy. Options: Source Serif, Lora, Tiempos.
Body: clean sans-serif. Inter, Source Sans, or similar.
The current all-sans setup reads as a SaaS startup. Serifs read as established and trustworthy — which is on-brand for Patti.
Imagery
Real photos of Patti. Replace every stock image — especially the duplicated “woman at laptop” shots.
If no pro shoot in budget, good iPhone photos in natural light outperform stock.
Supporting imagery: a clean ledger, a coffee cup, her actual workspace. Concrete, not metaphorical.
Drop the template SVG icons (clipboard, dollar sign, headset).
Layout
Generous whitespace. One idea per section.
Homepage should be scrollable once, scannable, and understood in a single viewport pass: who she is, who she helps, what she does, what to do next.
Mobile: fix the current duplicate hero/section bug. Verify on actual mobile.
